# Silkworm Rearing Environment

## 1. What It Is
The rearing environment comprises the physical and microclimatic conditions within the silkworm rearing house, primarily ambient temperature, relative humidity (RH), air velocity/ventilation, illumination, and airborne gaseous contaminants (e.g., carbon dioxide and ammonia).

## 2. Why It Matters
Silkworms are poikilothermic (cold-blooded) insects whose metabolic rate, enzyme activities, feed ingestion, digestion efficiency, and disease susceptibility are directly governed by external temperature and humidity. Improper ambient conditions during feed preparation or in the rearing bed cause feed to wilt excessively fast, leading to incomplete feed ingestion and disease outbreaks.

## 3. Key Concepts
The microclimatic optima established by the Central Silk Board (CSRTI Mysore benchmarks) across instars:

| Instar | Optimum Temperature (°C) | Optimum Relative Humidity (%) | Air Velocity | Primary Environmental Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1st Instar** | 27 – 28 °C | 85 – 90% | Low / Still (< 0.1 m/s) | Rapid feed desiccation; chilling |
| **2nd Instar** | 26 – 27 °C | 80 – 85% | Low (< 0.1 m/s) | Desiccation; starvation |
| **3rd Instar** | 25 – 26 °C | 75 – 80% | Moderate (0.2–0.3 m/s) | High humidity stagnation; fungal growth |
| **4th Instar** | 24 – 25 °C | 70 – 75% | Active (0.3–0.5 m/s) | Heat accumulation in beds; flacherie |
| **5th Instar** | 23 – 24 °C | 65 – 70% | High (0.5–1.0 m/s) | High bed moisture, fermentation, viral flares |

- **Ventilation**: Late instars generate substantial metabolic heat and excrete large volumes of feces (frass), producing ammonia and CO₂; active ventilation is essential to keep CO₂ below 0.1%.
- **Photoperiod**: 16 hours light : 8 hours dark or natural diurnal cycle; uniform gentle lighting ensures synchronized feeding behavior.

## 4. Engineering Relevance
- **Environmental Context of Cutting Operation**:
  - The smart cutting machine will typically operate either inside the rearing house anteroom or in an adjacent shaded leaf-storage room.
  - If operated in a hot, dry environment (> 30°C, < 50% RH), finely chopped leaves will lose crucial moisture in minutes. Machine cutting cycles must be timed immediately prior to scheduled feeding.
- **Dust and Particle Emissions**:
  - Mechanical chopping must not generate fine airborne particulate matter or vegetable dust that settles on silkworm beds or clogs spiracles (respiratory openings of larvae).
- **Machine Thermal Dissipation**:
  - Electric drive motors and onboard electronics dissipate heat. If the machine is positioned inside or near the rearing hall, localized hot spots must be avoided so as not to elevate room temperature above the 24–25°C limit during 5th instar rearing.
