# Silkworm Diseases and Biosecurity

## 1. What It Is
Silkworm diseases are infectious pathologies affecting *Bombyx mori* caused by protozoan microsporidia, viruses, bacteria, and entomopathogenic fungi. Biosecurity encompasses the preventive sanitation, disinfection, and quarantine measures implemented to prevent pathogens from entering the rearing room.

## 2. Why It Matters
A single outbreak of disease can destroy an entire rearing batch (100% crop loss) within 48 to 72 hours. Because silkworms are reared in high-density colonies in confined spaces, contaminated feed or pathogen transfer via equipment, blades, or human hands can rapidly trigger catastrophic epizootics.

## 3. Key Concepts
The four major diseases of commercial silkworms:

| Disease | Pathogen Class | Causative Agent | Symptoms | Transmission Route |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pebrine** | Microsporidian Protozoan | *Nosema bombycis* | Pepper-like black spots on skin, sluggish movement, uneven growth, pupal death | Transovarial (mother moth to egg) and oral (ingesting spores on feed) |
| **Grasserie** (Nuclear Polyhedrosis) | Double-stranded DNA Virus | *BmNPV* (Bombyx mori Nuclear Polyhedrosis Virus) | Swollen intersegmental membranes, shining milky white skin, fragile skin bursting milky hemolymph | Ingestion of polyhedra on contaminated leaf surface; contaminated equipment |
| **Flacherie** | Viral & Bacterial Complex | *BmIFV*, *BmDNV*, *Streptococcus*, *Bacillus* spp. | Flaccid, translucent bodies, digestive failure, vomiting gastric juice, foul-smelling putrefaction | Ingesting contaminated, fermented, dusty, or spoiled leaves |
| **Muscardine** (White/Green) | Entomopathogenic Fungus | *Beauveria bassiana* (White), *Spicaria prasina* (Green) | Body loses elasticity, turns limp, dies, mummifies, and becomes covered with chalky white/green fungal conidia | Cuticular penetration by fungal conidial spores; high humidity stagnation |

- **Sanitation Protocols**: Thorough disinfection of rearing houses and all appliances prior to each rearing using 2% formalin, 0.05% chlorine dioxide, or 0.5% bleaching powder solutions.

## 4. Engineering Relevance
- **The Machine as a Potential Disease Vector**:
  - If a cutting machine processes leaves that carry fungal spores, bacteria, or frass residues, its blades, feed rollers, belts, and collection chutes can harbor and transmit pathogens across consecutive feed batches.
- **Biosecurity & Washdown Architecture**:
  - The mechanical cutting unit must feature easy toolless disassembly for daily washdown.
  - Materials must withstand chemical disinfectants (chlorine bleach, formaldehyde, caustic soda) without corrosion, pitting, or surface degradation.
  - Elimination of blind crevices, dead zones, porous seals, and unsealed bearings where leaf pulp and sap can stagnate and ferment.
- **Automated Contamination / Biosecurity Detection (Proposed Research)**:
  - Exploring optical/vision sensing to identify visibly blighted, soiled, or fungus-infested leaves before they enter the cutting blades, preventing machine contamination. *(Status: Proposed concept; to be investigated experimentally).*
