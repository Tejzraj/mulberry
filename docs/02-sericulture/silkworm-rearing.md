# Silkworm Rearing Practices

## 1. What It Is
Silkworm rearing is the systematic husbandry of *Bombyx mori* from brushing of newly hatched larvae through feeding, bed cleaning, moult care, and spacing until maturity and cocoon spinning. It encompasses both young-age (Chawki) rearing and late-age rearing methods.

## 2. Why It Matters
Rearing management directly dictates cocoon harvest weight, survival rate, and disease incidence. The method of rearing chosen by a farmer (e.g., individual leaf tray rearing vs. shoot rearing) determines the physical format, volume, and presentation of the mulberry foliage required from the cutting machine.

## 3. Key Concepts
- **Chawki Rearing Practices**:
  - Conducted in specialized Chawki Rearing Centers (CRCs) or dedicated chambers.
  - Wooden or plastic trays lined with paraffin paper and covered with wet foam pads/paraffin paper to retain high humidity (> 80%).
  - Larvae require tender, bruise-free, chopped leaves scattered evenly across the bed.
- **Late-Age Rearing Methods**:
  1. *Shelf / Tray Rearing*: Larvae are reared in wooden or bamboo trays arranged on tiered rearing stands. Fed with plucked leaves or short cut shoots. Labor-intensive bed cleaning.
  2. *Floor Rearing*: Larvae are reared on cement or earthen floors in large continuous beds. Fed whole leaves or cut shoots.
  3. *Shoot Rearing (Shoot Feeding Method)*: Larvae are reared on elevated racks or floor beds and fed whole intact branches (shoots). Greatly reduces harvesting labor by 40–50% and improves bed ventilation, but requires robust branch handling and subsequent waste disposal.
- **Bed Cleaning**:
  - The periodic removal of accumulated uneaten leaf remnants, petioles, shed skins, and frass (silkworm excrement) using cleaning nets to preserve sanitation and prevent fungal infection.
  - Improperly cut feed (e.g., leaves bruised or clumped into large clumps) increases bed fouling and fungal growth.

## 4. Engineering Relevance
- **Input Biomass Formats**:
  - If supporting *Tray Rearing*: Machine must chop individual leaves or loose leaf-petiole mixtures cleanly into uniform rectangular/square pieces without mashing.
  - If supporting *Shoot Rearing*: Machine must handle whole woody branches (5–15 mm diameter) with leaves attached, either slicing stems into specific lengths or stripping/cutting foliage while rejecting hardened basal wood.
- **Feed Uniformity and Bed Aeration**:
  - Clumped or mashed leaves restrict airflow in rearing beds, promoting microclimates favorable to entomopathogens (such as *Beauveria bassiana* or nuclear polyhedrosis virus). Clean-cut, aerated feed maintains dry bed surface conditions.
- **Hygiene & Sanitation of the Machine**:
  - Silkworm pathogens (especially viruses and microsporidia) can be spread mechanically via contact surfaces. The cutting machine must be engineered from non-porous, corrosion-resistant, food-grade materials (e.g., stainless steel AISI 304 or food-grade polymers) that permit rapid washdown with disinfectants (0.5% bleaching powder or 2% formalin solutions).
