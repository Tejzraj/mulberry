# Silkworm Life Cycle (*Bombyx mori*)

## 1. What It Is
The domesticated silkworm, *Bombyx mori*, undergoes complete metamorphosis (holometabolism), comprising four distinct biological stages: egg, larva (caterpillar), pupa (enclosed within the silk cocoon), and adult moth (imago). The entire cycle spans approximately 45 to 55 days depending on ambient rearing temperature and genetic strain (univoltine, bivoltine, or multivoltine).

## 2. Why It Matters
Only the larval stage feeds on mulberry leaves. Over a period of 24 to 28 days, silkworm larvae consume enormous volumes of foliage, increasing their body weight roughly 10,000-fold from hatching to spinning. Understanding each developmental phase informs how feed biomass requirements, cutting sizes, and mechanical throughput demand shift throughout the rearing cycle.

## 3. Key Concepts
- **Egg Stage (Embryonic Phase)**:
  - Laid by female moths (~400–500 eggs per laying).
  - In diapause strains, chilled/acid-treated for commercial synchronization.
  - Incubation period is 10–12 days at 25°C and 75–80% RH until uniform "black boxing" and hatching.
- **Larval Stage (Feeding Phase)**:
  - Lasts 24–28 days across 5 distinct instars separated by 4 moults.
  - Exclusively phytophagous on mulberry; feeds actively during intermoult periods and ceases feeding entirely during ecdysis (moulting).
  - Accumulates nutrients to fuel cocoon spinning and metamorphic transformation.
- **Moulting (Ecdysis)**:
  - Silkworms shed their chitinous head capsule and cuticle 4 times to accommodate somatic growth.
  - During moult (lasting 20–24 hours each), larvae remain stationary, head elevated, and do not eat. Feeding during this period is detrimental and wasteful.
- **Pupal Stage (Silk Spinning & Metamorphosis)**:
  - Fully grown 5th instar larvae cease feeding, turn translucent, and climb to spin silk cocoons via two paired sericteries (silk glands) discharging liquid fibroin and sericin through the spinneret.
  - Spinning takes 48–72 hours. The larva transforms into a pupa within the protective cocoon.
  - Pupal duration is 10–14 days inside the cocoon before moth emergence.
- **Adult Moth (Reproductive Phase)**:
  - The adult moth escapes the cocoon by secreting cocoonase to soften the silk matrix.
  - Moths have rudimentary, non-functional mouthparts and do not feed; they mate, lay eggs, and perish within 3–7 days.

## 4. Engineering Relevance
- **Extreme Scale Shift in Feed Demand**:
  - Larval weight increases from ~0.45 mg at hatching to ~4.0–5.5 g at maturity (10,000× increase).
  - Feed consumption is non-linear: over 80–85% of total foliage is consumed during the 4th and 5th instars alone.
  - A cutting machine must cater to precision micro-cutting (0.5–2 cm pieces) for low-throughput early stages, yet switch to high-throughput shoot cutting or coarse fragmentation (several kg/minute) for mature larvae.
- **Synchronization with Feeding Schedules**:
  - Feeding occurs in strict schedules (typically 2 to 4 times per day). The machine must operate reliably during narrow feeding windows without prolonged setup or maintenance delays.

## 5. Questions to Investigate
- What is the daily feed consumption rate (in grams per 1,000 worms) across each day of larval development?
- How quickly does leaf degradation occur in rearing beds if cut sizes are mismatched to larval feeding speed?
- Does feeding frequency impact the required cutting batch size and peak machine duty cycle?
