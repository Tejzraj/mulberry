# Mulberry Cultivation

## 1. What It Is
Mulberry cultivation (moriculture) encompasses the agricultural practices required to establish, cultivate, prune, fertilize, and irrigate mulberry gardens to ensure continuous, high-volume production of nutritious leaves for silkworm batches.

## 2. Why It Matters
Agronomic management directly dictates shoot growth rate, stem diameter, fiber toughness, and leaf moisture levels. The physical dimensions and moisture state of the crop entering the cutting machine are direct products of the cultivation regime.

## 3. Key Concepts
- **Planting Systems**: Paired-row system (e.g., `(90 + 150) cm x 60 cm`) or pit systems (`90 cm x 90 cm` or `60 cm x 60 cm`) designed to facilitate mechanization, irrigation, and intercultural operations.
- **Pruning & Cropping Cycles**: Mulberry bushes are pruned 5 to 6 times per year under irrigated tropical conditions (such as Southern India), yielding a new harvest flush every 50 to 60 days.
- **Irrigation & Moisture**: Optimal leaf moisture (> 70%) requires 1.5 to 2.0 acre-inches of water every 8 to 10 days in dry seasons. Moisture directly impacts leaf turgidity and blade shear mechanics.
- **Soil & Nutrient Inputs**: Application of farmyard manure (FYM) and N:P:K chemical fertilizers (recommended doses around 350:140:140 kg/ha/yr for V-1 under irrigation, per CSRTI Mysore guidelines) promotes high nitrogenous vegetative growth and succulence.

## 4. Engineering Relevance
- **Stem Diameter Distribution at Harvest**: At the standard 50–60 day harvest interval, shoots typically exhibit basal diameters of 6–12 mm, tapering to 2–4 mm at the apex. Feed throats and cutters must accommodate this size range.
- **Batch Uniformity**: Paired-row mechanical harvesting or shoot harvests result in dense bundles of shoots with attached leaves, necessitating a mechanism that feeds bundles without tangling.
- **Moisture-Related Slip**: High sap and moisture content during peak growth can lead to slippery feeder rollers, requiring grooved, knurled, or rubberized feed traction.
