# Mulberry Varieties

## 1. What It Is
Sericultural research institutions have bred and selected high-yielding mulberry varieties (*Morus* cultivars) optimized for specific agro-climatic zones, irrigation regimens, leaf nutrition, and disease resistance.

## 2. Why It Matters
Different cultivars exhibit markedly different leaf thicknesses, petiole shear strengths, branch flexibilities, and growth postures. A cutting machine designed solely for thin, pliable leaves may stall or jam when processing thick, leathery, or fibrous varieties.

## 3. Key Concepts
- **Victory-1 (V-1)**: Developed by CSRTI Mysore; the premier variety in irrigated tropical sericulture (South India). Characterized by large, thick, oval, glossy dark green unlobed leaves, high moisture retention, and erect thick branches. Leaf yield ~60 tonnes/ha/year under optimal irrigation.
- **S-36**: Bred for chawki (young silkworm) rearing. Heart-shaped, light green, succulent leaves with exceptionally high moisture (~75–80%) and protein content. Stems are slightly softer and more pliable than V-1.
- **G-4**: High-yielding variety suited for intensive farming with rapid growth flushes and erect branches.
- **Kanva-2 (M-5)**: Historically widespread variety; hardy, medium leaf thickness, slightly more branching with moderate moisture retention.
- **Rainfed Varieties (e.g., S-13, RFS-175, Anantha)**: Adapted to water-stressed regions, having thicker cuticles, denser cell walls, and more fibrous stems to minimize transpirational water loss.

## 4. Engineering Relevance
- **Blade Wear & Cutting Force**: Cultivars with thicker cuticles and higher lignification (e.g., rainfed varieties) exert higher shear loads on cutting edges than chawki-specific cultivars (S-36).
- **Computer Vision Segmentation**: Variations in leaf shape (ovate in V-1 vs. cordate in S-36), leaf surface sheen (glossy vs. matte), and green shade require color and texture invariance in computer vision classifiers.
- **Material Elasticity**: Thicker V-1 leaves resist crushing better than thin cultivars, but their stiff petioles require clean shear rather than blunt impact tearing.
