# Cutting Mechanisms and Physics

## 1. What It Is
Cutting mechanisms are the physical assemblies and kinematics employed to apply localized mechanical shear, compression, and tension to biological materials to effect cellular separation along a defined plane.

## 2. Why It Matters
The chosen cutting physics determines the cut edge cleanliness, specific cutting energy ($J/m^2$ or $J/kg$), power requirements, mechanical vibration, acoustic noise, and rate of blade wear. In sericulture, clean shear separation without cellular crushing is essential to preserve leaf moisture.

## 3. Key Concepts & Mechanical Principles

### Cutting Mechanics Overview
- **Pure Shear Cutting (Scissor Action)**:
  - Two cutting edges moving in opposite directions past each other with minimal clearance ($c \approx 0.05 - 0.15 \text{ mm}$).
  - Characterized by localized shear deformation, minimal lateral compression, clean cellular cleavage, and lowest moisture expulsion.
- **Counter-Shear Against a Ledger Plate (Anvil Bar)**:
  - A moving sharp knife passes across a stationary hardened shear bar (ledger plate).
  - Most common in cylindrical drum choppers and forage cutters.
- **Impact Cutting (Single Free Blade)**:
  - High-velocity knife ($v > 20 - 30 \text{ m/s}$) impacting unconstrained or cantilevered stalks.
  - Requires high kinetic energy; causes severe shattering, bruising, and cell wall destruction. Unsuitable for early instar sericulture.

### Key Cutting Parameters
- **Bevel Angle ($\beta$)**: Angle of the blade cutting wedge. Typically 20°–30° for soft fibrous plant materials. Sharper angles reduce cutting force but chip more easily on woody stems.
- **Shear Clearance ($c$)**: Distance between the moving blade and stationary counter-edge. If clearance exceeds 0.2 mm, thin leaf laminae are pulled into the gap and chewed/folded rather than cut.
- **Cutting Velocity ($v_c$)**: Linear velocity of the blade edge at contact. In shear cutters, moderate velocities (1.5 to 5.0 m/s) provide clean cuts without excessive centrifugal air currents or bruising.
- **Oblique / Slicing Angle ($\phi$)**: Angle between the blade cutting edge and direction of motion. Slicing (oblique) action reduces peak cutting force by transforming normal compression into drawing shear.
- **Specific Cutting Energy ($E_s$)**: Mechanical work consumed per unit area of cross-section cut ($J/cm^2$) or per unit mass ($kJ/kg$). Varies drastically between succulent leaf laminae and lignified branch xylem.

## 4. Engineering Relevance
- **Geometry Comparison for Mulberry Processing**:

| Mechanism | Kinematics | Cleanliness of Cut | Jamming Tendency | Power Consumption | Complexity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cylindrical Rotary Drum Shear** | Helical blades mounted on rotating cylinder shearing against straight ledger bar. | High (if clearance maintained) | Low to Medium | Moderate | Medium |
| **Counter-Rotating Disc Shears** | Intermeshing circular disc blades with zero/low clearance. | Very High for flat leaves | High with tangled fibrous stems | Low | High |
| **Reciprocating Guillotine Shear** | Linear sliding blade driven by crank/slider mechanism against fixed bed. | Extremely High | Very Low | Low-Medium (pulsed) | Medium |
| **Oscillating Dual Cutter Bar** | Opposed reciprocating toothed knives (hedge-trimmer type). | Moderate | High with thick stems | Low | Medium |

- **Mechanism Selection Criteria**:
  - The rotary drum shear with continuous rotary feed offers the best combination of continuous feed throughput, uniform cut length control (by adjusting feed speed vs drum RPM), and low torque pulsation.
  - However, maintaining sub-0.1 mm blade clearance along a rotating drum across varying temperatures and mechanical deflection is an important mechanical design challenge.
